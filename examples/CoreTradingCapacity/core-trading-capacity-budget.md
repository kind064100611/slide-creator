# Global Compute Supply-Chain Pressure

## Lock capacity now to protect the expansion schedule

Geopolitical competition and structural capacity shifts have tightened high-end server supply. Without long-term agreements, inventory may be unavailable next year and the expansion schedule may slip.

### GPU compute capacity

- Delivery lead time is **52+ weeks**.
- Some constrained models will remain queued until the second half of 2027.
- NVIDIA allocations favor hyperscale cloud providers, leaving enterprises with limited direct supply.
- Capacity is **severely constrained**.

### HBM memory

- The supply gap is **50-60%**.
- Three major suppliers shifted more than 40% of advanced DRAM capacity to HBM.
- The structural shift has sharply reduced general high-performance memory supply.
- The result is a **structural shortage**.

### CPU and spot market

- Secondary-market markup is **>50%**.
- Some Intel Xeon processors have lead times of up to six months and prices more than 10% higher.
- Shortages affect the full chain, and spot-market premiums remain severe.
- The result is **extreme premium risk**.

# Supply-Chain Risk Response and Redundancy

### 01 Multiple qualified suppliers

- Avoid single-supplier dependence for critical parts such as CPUs.
- Prequalify vendors so supply can switch after a disruption.

### 02 Safety-stock mechanism

- Maintain safety stock for critical components.
- Hold three months of vulnerable parts and modules.

### 03 Elastic scaling and recovery

- Use autoscaling to prevent overload during traffic surges.
- Maintain off-site recovery with rapid failover.

### 04 Long-term supply agreements

- Reserve future capacity through take-or-pay agreements.
- Reduce price-volatility and allocation risk.

## Summary

Forward-looking supply measures reduce uncertainty. They create a resilient IT foundation for stable operations.
